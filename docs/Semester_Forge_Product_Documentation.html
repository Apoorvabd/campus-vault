The idea that is easy to miss

Semester Forge is not a collection of PDFs. It is an academic-context-aware student platform: it knows who the student is academically, and brings the right resources to them.

01 · Executive summary

What Semester Forge is, in one page

Semester Forge (also called Campus Vault) is a mobile app for Delhi University students. A student tells the app their University → College → Course → Semester once. From then on, the app shows the subjects and study resources that belong to their semester — previous year question papers (PYQs), notes, syllabus and more — and lets them search, save, contribute and discuss.

02 · The problem

Students don't need more PDFs. They need to stop hunting for them.

Delhi University students find study material in many places at once. The material exists — but it is scattered, unverified, duplicated and hard to remember.

Current reality: where material lives today

WhatsApp groupsTelegram & other student groupsGoogle Drive linksRandom websitesCollege-specific pagesUniversity portalsOld Question Paper Bank linksPersonal collectionsSenior / junior groups
The deeper problem

It is not simply "students need PDFs." Students have to find, verify, organize and remember where their academic material is — every semester, for every subject, again and again.

1

Find

Which group, which drive, which website has my subject's paper? Searching is manual and repeated.

2

Verify

Is this the right paper, the right year, the right course? Files are renamed, reshared and duplicated.

3

Organize

Everything lives in chat history or scattered folders, not in the structure a student actually studies in.

4

Remember

A week before exams, nobody remembers where they saw that file. Links also break (see the DU portal case below).

Same resource, different student

Students differ by college, course, semester and subjects. A resource that is useful to one student is noise for another. Generic "download PDFs" sites cannot know this; a student has to filter everything by hand.

04 · Product philosophy

Bring the resources to the student

"Students should not have to search for academic resources every time. The application should understand what they study and bring the relevant resources to them."

It is more than a PDF repository

Semester Forge combines four things that are usually separate:

Academic resource discoveryfind the right paper or notes

Academic organizationby course, semester, subject
＋
Communityposts, likes, comments
＋
Student contributionupload and review
＝
Academic-context-aware student platform

Principles visible in the product

Identity first

The student's university, college, course, semester and subject picks decide what appears on Home and in "Your Subjects".

Two trust models

Project docs describe a Vault (resources: many contributors, moderated, high trust) and a Feed (posts and reactions: open conversation). They share one app but different rules.

Link-first resources

A resource can be a hosted file, an external link, or a reference only. Today every seeded resource is an external link, which keeps hosting cost at zero but makes link health important.

05 · Who it is for

Target users

Delhi University students ● Primary audience

Undergraduates from first year onward. Each student has a college, a course (118 courses are catalogued), a current semester and, optionally, a confirmed list of subjects for that semester (core DSC, GE, DSE, SEC, VAC, AEC).

Contributors ● Available

Any signed-in student can submit a resource and publish a post. The README describes contributor reputation (points for approved uploads) as part of the vision; the current app shows a badges screen as a design preview only.

Reviewers and admins ◐ API only

The database has four roles: STUDENT, FACULTY, UNIVERSITY_ADMIN, SUPER_ADMIN. Users with an admin role can approve or reject resources through the API. There is no admin screen in the mobile app, and no endpoint to change a user's role. The FACULTY role exists in the schema but has no specific behavior.

Project team and maintainers

The repository contains the Flutter app, the API, seed data for the DU catalog, and documents describing plans. This document is written so that a new contributor can understand all of it.

06 · How it works

From sign-up to the right resource, in four ideas

1

Tell it who you are

At sign-up the student picks university → college → course → semester. The lists come from the server.

2

Confirm your subjects

Right after registering, the app asks for the subjects they take this semester (core, elective, skill, value-added, ability, generic elective).

3

See what is relevant

Home shows the student's profile and their subjects. Search finds approved resources. The feed shows what other students post.

4

Keep and contribute

Bookmark resources and posts, keep a private copy on the phone, upload resources for review, and publish posts.

Step by step

  1. Launch. A splash screen shows the logo while the app checks for a saved login. No saved login → Landing. Saved login → the app asks the server "who am I?" and, if that succeeds, goes straight to Home.
  2. Register. Login → "Register with email" → step 1 (name, optional username, email, password) → step 2 (university, college, course, semester, accept terms).
  3. Subjects setup. After registering, a "Your subjects" form opens over Home. Core subjects come pre-filled; the student adds electives and saves, or skips and finishes later from Profile.
  4. Home. A profile card (name, course, semester, college) and two tabs: Community (the feed) and Resources ("Your Subjects").
  5. Subject. Tapping a subject shows what the student has kept for it (bookmarked resources and files saved on the phone), with type filters. Browsing everything uploaded for a subject happens through Search.
  6. Open and save. "Open" launches the file or link outside the app (browser or PDF viewer) and increases the resource's download count. The bookmark button saves it to Saved.
  7. Search. The search box on every main screen opens a search page with recent searches, filters (type, semester, "my course", sort) and paged results.
  8. Community. Read posts, like, comment, bookmark, and delete your own posts. Create a post (title, text, optional photo) from the Add tab.
  9. Contribute. The Add tab → classify the resource (semester, subject, type) → upload a file or paste a link → submit. It is saved as Pending for review.
  10. Saved. One place for bookmarked resources, saved posts and files kept on the device.
  11. Profile. Stats, edit details and photo, change semester, re-confirm subjects, open settings pages, log out.

Rules that shape the experience

  • Login decides the screen. A listener on the authentication state sends the user to Home when logged in and to Landing when logged out — from anywhere in the app, including when a session expires.
  • Tabs replace each other. Switching tabs rebuilds the destination and reloads its data. Scroll positions and filters reset; nothing is cached between visits.
  • Detail screens stack. Subject detail, search, upload and settings pages open on top and have a back button.
  • Search is global. The top bar search field (home, saved, profile, add, subject) opens the Search page with a short fade.

A Subject belongs to a Course and carries an optional semester number. A Resource belongs to a Subject. Lifecycles

Discovering a resource

  1. Student finds it by search (or in Saved).
  2. Taps Open — the file or link opens in the browser or PDF viewer.
  3. The app tells the API (download count +1).
  4. Optionally taps the bookmark to keep it in Saved.

Submitting a resource

  1. Student chooses semester, subject and type, adds title and a file or link.
  2. API checks the subject, uploads the file if any, saves the record as Pending.
  3. An admin approves or rejects it (API call).
  4. If approved, it becomes discoverable by everyone.

PasswordsStored only as a bcrypt hash (10 rounds); minimum length 8.
TokensTwo JWTs signed with different secrets. Payload: user id, email, role. Lifetimes come from settings (defaults 15 minutes and 7 days).
Where tokens are keptOn the phone in secure storage (Keychain / Keystore) under access_token and refresh_token. No cookies.
Authenticated requestsEvery request carries Authorization: Bearer <access token>, attached automatically by the app's network layer.
Server checkA middleware verifies the token, loads the user (cached in memory for 30 seconds), rejects unknown (404) or deactivated (403) users, and attaches the user to the request. A second middleware makes login optional for public lists; a third checks admin roles.
RefreshOn a 401 the app calls POST /auth/refresh-token once (even if several requests failed together), saves the new pair, then repeats the failed request. The server issues a brand-new token pair.

Session expiryIf refresh fails, tokens are cleared and the user is sent to Landing automatically.
LogoutThe app calls POST /auth/logout (which does nothing on the server) and always clears its own tokens.

Security notes

Refresh tokens are stateless (no revocation list), so a stolen one works until it expires. If the token secrets are not set in the server settings, the code falls back to built-in placeholder values — they must be set in every deployment. Login does not check whether an account is deactivated (later requests do). There is no email verification, password reset or Google sign-in yet (the buttons exist). See Security model.

Response envelope

Success: { success: true, message, data, meta? }, where meta holds paging details. Error: { success: false, message }. Validation problems are joined into one readable message with status 400.

Error handling

A global handler turns known problems into clean answers: duplicates → 409, missing record → 404, bad token → 401, expired token → 401, validation → 400. Anything else becomes 500 "Internal server error".

Paging

Cursor paging for posts and comments (stable for live feeds); page/limit paging for resources and bookmarks.

Performance choices

Relations are fetched with SQL joins (one query instead of several); the logged-in user is cached for 30 seconds; the database connection is opened at start-up and kept warm; resource search uses trigram indexes.

Settings (names only)

The API reads its configuration from environment variables: PORT, DATABASE_URL, CLIENT_URL, JWT_ACCESS_SECRET, JWT_REFRESH_SECRET, JWT_ACCESS_EXPIRES_IN, JWT_REFRESH_EXPIRES_IN, CLOUDINARY_CLOUD_NAME, CLOUDINARY_API_KEY, CLOUDINARY_API_SECRET (the data-collection script also uses DU portal variables). Values are never stored in the repository.

24 · Example request flows

Nine journeys, layer by layer

1 · Login

  1. App: LoginSubmitted → AuthRepository.
  2. HTTP: POST /auth/login {email, password}.
  3. Server: validate → find user by email → compare bcrypt hash → create access + refresh tokens (user without password hash).
  4. App: saves tokens → Authenticated → Home, and loads the profile.

2 · Fetch Home data

  1. App: after login ProfileCubit calls GET /profile/me; the feed calls GET /posts; the Resources tab calls GET /subjects/mine.
  2. Server: authenticate (cached user) → profile with university, college, course and counters in one joined query.
  3. App: Home card shows the profile; tabs fill when their data arrives.

3 · Subjects and resources

  1. App: Resources tab → GET /subjects/mine.
  2. Server: if the student confirmed picks for this semester → those subjects; else all active subjects of the course for the semester, sorted by type then name.
  3. App: tile list. A subject page then reads the student's bookmarks (GET /bookmarks/resources) and device files.

4 · Search

  1. App: debounce → GET /resources?search=…&sort=downloads&page=1&limit=20.
  2. Server: validate query → approved only → title/description match → filters → sort → page + count, with subject, uploader and bookmark state.
  3. App: result cards; opening one calls POST /resources/:id/download.

5 · Save (bookmark) a resource

  1. App: bookmark icon → instant UI change.
  2. HTTP: POST /bookmarks/resources/:id.
  3. Server: authenticate → resource must be approved (or yours) → insert once (idempotent) → return the new count.
  4. App: keeps the change; on error restores the icon.

6 · Create a community post

  1. App: CreatePostCubit → POST /posts (multipart).
  2. Server: authenticate → canPost check (else 403) → Multer reads the image → validate title/content → upload cover to Cloudinary → save the post.
  3. App: closes the screen; feed shows it on reload.

7 · Like and comment

  1. Like: POST /posts/:id/like → upsert one like per user → return likesCount.
  2. Comment: POST /posts/:id/comments {content} → validate (≤ 5,000) → insert → return the comment with the author's name.
  3. App: list and counts update; the sheet reports the net comment change to the feed.

8 · Upload a resource

  1. App: UploadCubit validates (title, subject, type, file ≤ 15 MB or link) → POST /resources (multipart).
  2. Server: Multer → validate fields → subject exists? → if hosted, upload to Cloudinary and save an Upload row → create the resource as Pending.
  3. App: "Submitted for review"; form resets.

9 · Update the profile

  1. App: Edit sheet → PATCH /profile/me (only changed fields).
  2. Server: validate (names, username pattern, lengths) → username free? → semester ≤ course length? → update → clear the cached user → return the new profile.
  3. App: ProfileCubit replaces the profile (counters kept); Home and Profile redraw.

28 · Security model

What protects the system — and what does not yet

In place ● Available

  • Passwords stored as bcrypt hashes (10 rounds); never returned by the API.
  • Short-lived access tokens plus refresh tokens; tokens kept in the phone's secure storage.
  • Every protected route checks the token and that the account is active.
  • Admin-only routes check the role.
  • Input validated with Zod on every write; owners only can edit or delete their posts and comments.
  • Public lists hide non-approved resources; uploader or admin only can see them.
  • Uploads restricted by type and size.

Needs attention ● Open

  • No rate limiting or security headers; no brute-force protection on login.
  • Token secrets fall back to built-in placeholders if not configured.
  • Refresh tokens cannot be revoked; logout does nothing on the server.
  • Login does not check for deactivated accounts.
  • Uploaded file URLs are public; uploads are checked by declared type only.
  • The test upload route is still mounted; the database client logs every query.
  • Admin approval is not limited to the admin's own university.

35 · Final summary

Everything in one place

Semester Forge is not merely a collection of PDFs. It is a structured academic platform that understands a student's university, college, course, semester and subjects, organizes academic resources around that context, and combines resource discovery with student contribution and community.

QuestionShort answer
What is Semester Forge?A Flutter mobile app for Delhi University students that organizes study resources and a student feed around their course and semester.
Why was it built?Because study material is scattered across chats, drives, websites and portals, and students waste time finding, verifying and remembering it.
What problem does it solve?It replaces manual hunting with "my college → my course → my semester → my subject → relevant resources".
Who uses it?Delhi University students; contributors among them; reviewers/admins through the API.
How does a student use it?Register with their academic identity → confirm subjects → browse subjects, search, open and save resources → post, comment, upload → manage the profile.
What does every screen do?See Screen-by-screen — 25 screens and sheets, each with purpose, actions, data, API and status.
What features exist?Registration, login, subjects setup, Your Subjects, search, bookmarks, uploads for review, community feed, profile, files on the device. See Feature status.
How does the resource system work?Resources belong to subjects; have a type, a source (file/link/reference) and a review status; only approved ones are public. See Resource system.
How does the community work?A feed of posts with optional photos; anyone with posting permission can post; everyone can like, comment and bookmark. See Community.
How does the backend work?An Express/TypeScript REST API in layers (routes, middleware, controllers, services, repositories) over Prisma. See Backend.
How does the database work?PostgreSQL with users, an academic catalog (university → college/course → subject), resources, posts, comments, likes, bookmarks and uploads. See Database.
How does authentication work?bcrypt-hashed passwords; short-lived access tokens plus refresh tokens kept in secure storage; automatic refresh; stateless logout. See Authentication.
How are files stored?Photos and hosted files go through the API to Cloudinary; the database keeps the URLs; saved device files stay on the phone. See Files & Cloudinary.
What works today?The core flows listed above run against real data. About 65% of old DU links are dead; some screens are previews.
What are the main limitations?Dead DU links, no admin screens, security hardening still to do, no tests, no offline cache. See Known issues.
What is the broader capability?Exam Mode, notifications, contributor reputation, moderation and verification, built on the same academic context. See Product vision.
How is everything connected?Flutter app → REST API → PostgreSQL and Cloudinary, with the student's academic identity deciding what each screen shows. See System architecture.
